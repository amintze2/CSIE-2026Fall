Opportunity Clusters

A cluster is a place where several students independently converged on the same opportunity space, usually while exploring different domains. No one planned the overlap, and that is why it counts: when the same problem keeps coming up in separate explorations, it is probably bigger than any one domain.

How to read the signals:
Strong Signal = 4+ students across 2+ domains
Emerging = 3-4 students
Early = 2-3 students
Convergence across independent explorations is a strong validation signal.
Week 2 Clusters

Strong Signal AI Outgrows the Institutions Built to Govern It

12 students, 3 domains

Three of the four Week 2 teams, working separately, ended up at the same question from different directions. Public Approval of AI saw use outrunning legitimacy. Social Media saw courts deciding that recommendation design itself is the harm. AI Media saw generation outrunning anyone's ability to say what is real or who answers for it. The surface domains (public opinion, algorithms, entertainment) look unrelated, but all seven of their opportunities sit in the gap between an AI system's scale and the institution meant to govern it.

Strong Signal Proof of Origin at Every Handoff

12 students, 3 domains (+ Week 1 Legal)

Smart Cities' trust layer records chain of custody at every transfer because goods move faster than a human can check them. AI Media's provenance gap asks for attestation at capture because synthetic media is indistinguishable from recorded media. Social Media's action layer puts a verified-source and motive label on a clip before it plays. All three are the same move: attach a verifiable origin record to something that arrives faster than anyone can inspect it. Week 1's Legal verified-filing certification is a fourth instance.

Strong Signal Liability With No Holder

8 students, 2 domains (+ Week 1 Legal)

Public Approval's AI tracker exists because responsibility falls between the model vendor, the deployer, and the approver. AI Media's accountability vacuum exists because machine-scale publishing outpaces review while liability law still assumes a publisher who did the reviewing. Both teams concluded that unpriced risk blocks adoption. Week 1's Legal opportunity already showed the business model: verify the AI output and sell the verification to whoever underwrites the risk.

Strong Signal Convenience's Costs Land Locally

8 students, 2 domains

Week 2 Connections

One Chain-of-Custody Standard for Parcels and Pixels

Smart Cities / Rapid Delivery trust and verification layer <--> AI-Generated Media and Entertainment provenance gap

Establishing whether an instantly delivered package is what it claims to be has the same structure as establishing whether a video is what it claims to be. In both, the object arrives faster than anyone can inspect it, and the fix is a signed record written at each handoff. AI Media diagnosed that "what's missing is a standard and a first adopter." Delivery fleets already log every transfer and have regulators asking for exactly that record, which makes them a plausible first adopter for an attestation standard that later extends to cameras.

Built from: Yash Chadda, Sichen Deng, Kendall Frederick, Jonathan Locala (Smart Cities); Daniel Sung, Flora Huang, Steven Li, Owen Bartlett (AI Media)

Motive Labels That Can Be Audited

Addictive and Opaque Social Media Algorithms verified-source and motive labels on clips <--> AI-Generated Media and Entertainment authenticity premium certification

Social Media's label ("paid political ad," "advocacy campaign") and AI Media's authenticity certification are both claims about a piece of content's origin. Both fail the same way: the claim is self-asserted and nothing separates an honest label from a cheap one. AI Media's point that durable margin goes to whoever owns the audit applies directly. A single audited-claims layer could certify both "human-made" and "who paid for this."

Built from: Jingchen Jiang, Sahana Raja, Sophia Mai, Sylvia Yam (Social Media); Daniel Sung, Flora Huang, Steven Li, Owen Bartlett (AI Media)

Large-Load Capacity Exchange

Public Approval of AI who pays for the power <--> Smart Cities / Rapid Delivery logistics real-estate exchange

Smart Cities proposed a market for reserving and pricing scarce curb, rooftop, and skylane access. Public Approval found the same scarcity on the grid: capacity built for one data center, paid for by every ratepayer. Apply the exchange model to large-load interconnection so that capacity is reserved and priced up front by the facility that needs it, instead of being socialized onto household bills after the fact. That also answers the payer question the Public Approval team left open.

Built from: Sissi Lai, Kerry Wang, Penn Riney, Evan Batten (Public Approval of AI); Yash Chadda, Sichen Deng, Kendall Frederick, Jonathan Locala (Smart Cities)

The Tracker Feeds the Underwriter

Public Approval of AI AI tracker (accountability) <--> AI-Generated Media and Entertainment accountability vacuum

AI Media observed that liability for machine-scale publishing can't be priced without loss data, and loss data only exists after claims. Public Approval's AI tracker, which records who built, deployed, and approved an AI system, is exactly the attribution record an underwriter needs to price that risk before the claims arrive. The tracker could be the data asset and the insurance product the business built on it.

Built from: Sissi Lai, Kerry Wang, Penn Riney, Evan Batten (Public Approval of AI); Daniel Sung, Flora Huang, Steven Li, Owen Bartlett (AI Media)

Simulated Apprenticeship

Public Approval of AI junior-to-senior transition <--> Defense and National Security Week 1 digital-twin ICS cyber range

Nobody becomes senior without supervised, low-stakes work, and AI is removing that work. The Week 1 digital twin replays real system data so tools can be tested without touching the real ship. Use the same method to give junior engineers a realistic replica of production where they can break things, get reviewed, and build judgment, which recreates the on-ramp AI closed.

Built from: Sissi Lai, Kerry Wang, Penn Riney, Evan Batten (Public Approval of AI); Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security, Week 1)

Scroll-to-Doorstep

Addictive and Opaque Social Media Algorithms actionable short-form layer <--> Smart Cities / Rapid Delivery instant fulfillment / neighborhood network

Social Media's action layer already includes "recipe clip, add to grocery list." In Smart Cities' 10-minute world, that list doesn't need to wait for a shopping trip. Connecting the action layer to hyperlocal fulfillment turns a passive scroll into delivered groceries or workout gear. It also shows the tension: a feed that sells more easily may be one users want to optimize away from.

Built from: Jingchen Jiang, Sahana Raja, Sophia Mai, Sylvia Yam (Social Media); Yash Chadda, Sichen Deng, Kendall Frederick, Jonathan Locala (Smart Cities)

Declared-Demand Pre-Positioning

Smart Cities / Rapid Delivery goods moving before explicit orders <--> Addictive and Opaque Social Media Algorithms user-set feed goals

Smart Cities' absurd world moves inventory on inferred demand. Social Media found that inferred preference misreads what people actually want. Combine them: let households declare standing intent ("keep me stocked on these," "never pre-send that") and pre-position inventory on declared demand rather than on behavioral guesses. That means fewer wasted deliveries, and consent is designed in from the start.

Built from: Yash Chadda, Sichen Deng, Kendall Frederick, Jonathan Locala (Smart Cities); Jingchen Jiang, Sahana Raja, Sophia Mai, Sylvia Yam (Social Media)

Gating Machine-Scale Publishing

AI-Generated Media and Entertainment accountability vacuum <--> Accessibility and Assistive Technology Week 1 confidence-gated AAC channel

The AAC channel won't commit an utterance until a confidence layer clears it. Machine-scale publishing has no such gate, which is exactly why nobody will absorb its tail risk. Put a confidence gate in front of publication: low-confidence output goes to human review and the rest ships. That gives the insurer a controllable, measurable process to price instead of an unbounded one.

Built from: Daniel Sung, Flora Huang, Steven Li, Owen Bartlett (AI Media); Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility, Week 1)

Neighborhood Readability Feed

Smart Cities / Rapid Delivery peer-to-peer neighborhood fulfillment <--> Travel Week 1 AI-readability layer for small operators

Peer-to-peer fulfillment only works if the network can see what every household has and when it can hand it off. That is the same problem as Week 1's Travel readability layer, a standard feed that makes small, scattered operators legible to one AI system. The same standard could serve households as nodes in a neighborhood warehouse.

Built from: Yash Chadda, Sichen Deng, Kendall Frederick, Jonathan Locala (Smart Cities); Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel, Week 1)

Week 1 Connections

Distributed Ground-Truth Mesh for Conflict/Disaster-Zone Travel

Travel AI-readability layer for small destinations <--> Defense and National Security cheap radar/interceptor mesh

Travel's readability layer assumes the underlying ground truth (prices, availability) is stable and just needs to be made legible to AI. In unstable regions it isn't stable -- conditions change hour to hour. Borrow Defense's move (many cheap distributed sensors, fused by software, replacing one expensive centralized system) and apply it to safety data instead of radar: a mesh of low-cost, locally-placed reporting nodes that gives AI trip-planners and travelers real-time ground truth on conflict, disaster, or unrest zones that no single "readable" feed could keep current.

Built from: Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel), Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security)

Accessible Crisis & Court Navigator

Accessibility and Assistive Technology confidence-gated AAC / real-time captioning <--> Legal pretrial reminder and needs-triage

Owen's pretrial triage service reminds people of court dates and routes them to what they need instead of a bail bond. But a deaf, hard-of-hearing, or communication-disabled defendant is disproportionately likely to miss exactly this kind of time-critical, high-stakes interaction in the first place. Wire the AAC/captioning group's confidence-gated, real-time-speed communication channel directly into the triage service's court-facing interactions -- so the accessibility layer isn't a generic add-on, it's what makes the triage service actually reach the population most likely to fall through the gap it was built to close.

Built from: Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility and Assistive Technology), Owen Bartlett (Legal)

Travel-Disruption Rights Autofiler

Legal AI-hallucination-verified filing certification <--> Travel cross-carrier rebooking during disruption

The moment the rebooking layer detects a disruption (cancellation, denied boarding, a carrier-caused delay), that event is very often also a compensation-eligible legal claim (DOT/EU261-style) that almost no traveler actually files, because doing so requires legal knowledge they don't have and time they don't want to spend. Attach a verified-filing service, built the same way as the malpractice-insurer certification (AI drafts the claim, a verification layer checks it against the actual rule and the rebooking event data before anything is submitted), so the rebooking product doesn't just get you rebooked -- it auto-files what you're owed.

Built from: Owen Bartlett (Legal), Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel)

Procurement-Grade Verified-Simulation Certification

Legal AI-hallucination-verified filing certification <--> Defense and National Security digital-twin ICS cyber range

This week's Defense questions kept circling the same pain point: procurement moves slower than the battlefield because no one can cheaply prove an autonomous system is safe before the RFP. Legal's insurers already accept an AI-verification layer in place of manual review for filings. Package the digital-twin cyber range the same way -- as a pre-certification product an autonomy vendor buys before bidding, the way a law firm buys hallucination-verification before it sends a filing -- so contracting officers can accept "independently verified in simulation" instead of re-deriving trust from scratch on every bid.

Built from: Owen Bartlett (Legal), Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security)

Mixed-Ability Collaborative Trip Planning

Travel social/collaborative group itineraries <--> Accessibility and Assistive Technology: spatial audio, gesture input

No one has pitched this pairing yet, but it's an obvious gap: Travel's collaborative-itinerary tool assumes every group member interacts with the plan the same way. Feed the Accessibility group's spatial-audio scene translation and tremor-tolerant gesture input in as first-class collaborative inputs -- a blind or motor-impaired traveler doesn't get a bolted-on accessibility mode, they get an equal vote in a shared itinerary that already speaks their input/output format.

Current contributors: Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel); Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility and Assistive Technology). Watch for someone to explicitly claim this connection.

Degraded-Input Operator Consoles

Defense and National Security digital-twin cyber range <--> Accessibility and Assistive Technology: tremor-tolerant gesture/switch input

Field the accessibility group's input-tolerance research as combat-console UX, validated in the same digital-twin cyber range before it's trusted in the real system.

Live-Captioned Debt & Eviction Hearings

Legal attorney-supervised AI defense for mass debt/eviction cases <--> Accessibility and Assistive Technology real-time conversational captioning

Owen's AI defense service represents defendants at a scale no attorney could match one-on-one, but the hearing itself is still spoken, real-time, and unforgiving of a missed word. Wire the Accessibility group's low-latency captioning directly into the hearing, feeding the live transcript straight into the AI defense pipeline as it happens -- so a deaf or hard-of-hearing defendant isn't just represented on paper, they're following their own case in real time.

Built from: Owen Bartlett (Legal), Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility and Assistive Technology)

Battlefield Spatial-Audio Threat Awareness

Defense and National Security radar/interceptor sensor-fusion mesh <--> Accessibility and Assistive Technology scene-to-spatial-audio translation

The spatial-audio work was built so a blind traveler can hear the direction and intensity of something they can't see. An operator in smoke, at night, or under EMCON needs exactly the same eyes-free situational read on what the radar/interceptor mesh is fusing. Repurpose the same translation layer -- direction and intensity as sound, not a screen -- as a hands-free, eyes-free threat channel for the operator, not the traveler.

Built from: Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security), Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility and Assistive Technology)

Digital-Twin Destination Simulator

Defense and National Security digital-twin ICS cyber range <--> Travel AI-readability layer for small destinations

Defense turns captured ship data into virtual device actors so scanners can be validated without ever touching the real hardware. Do the same thing before a small destination goes live on the readability feed: replay real AI trip-planning-agent query patterns against a simulated version of the destination's data, and catch the blunders and hallucinations in the twin before an actual traveler ever sees a bad recommendation.

Built from: Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security), Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel)

The Overlooked-Party Legibility Engine

Legal AI defense for overlooked debt/eviction defendants <--> Travel AI-readability layer for overlooked destinations

Strip away the domain and both opportunities are the same engine: take a party too small, too poor, or too obscure to earn individual institutional attention, and give it a standardized way to become legible and actionable to an AI system operating at scale. That's not just a two-domain connection -- it's a reusable pattern. The open question for the class: what's the third instantiation? A gig worker invisible to benefits systems, a small clinic invisible to insurance billing, a rural clinic invisible to a hospital network -- the mechanism built twice this week is generic enough to be built a third time, in a domain nobody's explored yet.

Built from: Owen Bartlett (Legal), Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel)

Redundant Multi-Vendor Defense Supply Mesh

Travel cross-carrier rebooking during disruption <--> Defense and National Security cheap radar/interceptor mesh

Travel's rebooking layer pre-purchases refundable capacity across multiple carriers specifically so no single airline's disruption can strand the traveler. This week's Defense questions asked, in effect, the same thing about hardware: what happens if a supplier's drones get phased out, or can't scale with the battlefield. Apply Travel's pre-arranged, swappable, multi-vendor capacity model to munitions/parts sourcing -- a logistics layer that treats supplier concentration as the risk to be engineered out, the same way single-airline lock-in already has been.

Built from: Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel), Group 3 -- Sahana, Kerry, Michael, Penn, Evan (Defense and National Security)

Group-Confidence-Gated Itinerary Commits

Accessibility and Assistive Technology confidence-gated AAC channel <--> Travel social/collaborative group itineraries

AAC won't commit an utterance to the conversation until a confidence layer says it's ready -- it protects against acting too early on an ambiguous signal. Travel's collaborative itinerary tool has no equivalent protection at the moment that actually costs money: locking in a non-refundable booking the instant one group member clicks. Borrow the gate: aggregate the whole group's confidence before the irreversible commit fires, not just the fastest clicker's.

Built from: Jingchen Jiang, Kendall Frederick, Sissi Lai, Sophia Mai (Accessibility and Assistive Technology), Sylvia, Steven, Flora, Yash (Travel)
